Transports durables



The development and extension of public transport networks were one of the most important components of the Paris Games transportation plan.

With this expectation, it is no surprise the preparations are being reinforced by constructing, extending, and even repurposing existing metro, Regional-Express lines, trans, and bus lines accordingly for hosting so many passengers. Somelinks on the RER "B" and "C" were vital in the coming games: the Stade de France, located near the Charles De Gaulle Airport, was central, while the hub lies between Olympic Village and thus with central Paris. Also recently opened, new metro stations enhance accessibility of Olympic venues while giving full assurance for the smooth procession of spectators. Beyond developing the existing transport means, Paris made a strong effort to promote eco-friendly modes of transport. The city used hydrogen-powered buses to make public transportation greener. Hydrogen-powered vehicles were part of a larger effort to switch towards zero-emission transport, which is in line with the long-term goals of Paris to become a carbon-neutral city.

In addition, wide cycling lanes were made available through the city so that the spectators had an easy, nonpolluting way of getting about and reducing traffic congestion.

Not to be overlooked was transport that was exclusively electric-drive in nature for all event-related needs. Many of the official cars, right from athlete transportation to very important personas attending the venues, have electric mobility in order to minimize emissions throughout these Games. And Paris went a mile further by coming up with many more charging stations on the roads not only during these Games but as permanent features of the city, to also allow residents and visitors greater scope for adaptation to much cleaner transport modes. Looking ahead, the sustainability of the transport network developed for the Paris Games is set to have long-lasting benefits. Many of the enhancements made to the city's infrastructure will remain, and the increased use of green public transport options like hydrogen buses and electric vehicles will be expanded

The Games left behind a green legacy in the aspect of green transport innovation that could inspire future Olympic host cities, such as Los Angeles, to emulate similar practices with a view to ensuring that their transport systems are both sustainable and efficient. Focusing on sustainability and reduction of emissions, Paris 2024 met the immediate transport demand for the Olympic Games and set a foundation to be followed for an eco-friendly transport system in the city for years to come.
